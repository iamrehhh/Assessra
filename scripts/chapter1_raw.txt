Political and Legal
Privatisation
It is the process of transferring ownership and control of government-owned businesses to private sector investors.
Advantages and Disadvantages of Privatisation

Advantages	Disadvantages
Greater efficiency	The state should make important decisions on essential industries to protect the needs of society
Decision-making is faster	Competing privately owned businesses are unlikely to achieve a unified policy for the country's benefit.
Greater motivation and empowerment than in state-owned businesses.	An industry can be made accountable to the country through state ownership
Market forces will operate, forcing failing businesses to change and giving successful ones the opportunity to expand	Strategic industries could be operated as private monopolies to exploit consumers with high prices
Important business decisions are taken for financial rather than political reasons	Breaking up nationalised industries reduces the opportunity to save costs through economies of scale
Finance for state projects can be raised through the sale of nationalised industries	-
Private capital markets can be used and will lead to increased investment in these industries	-
Nationalisation
It is the process of transferring privately owned businesses into state ownership and control
Advantages and Disadvantages of Nationalisation

Advantages	Disadvantages
Government will have control of major and essential industries	Inefficiency due to profit motive being absent
Integrated industrial policy can now be possible	Government may excessively interfere in business decisions for political reasons
Prevents exploitation of consumers	May be costly for the government to buy private companies
Economies of scale are now possible	Finance cannot be raised through private sources
